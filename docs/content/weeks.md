## First trimester

**Week 4** · a poppy seed (khus khus)  
Baby: The tiny embryo has settled into the lining of your womb and the placenta is starting to form.  
You: A missed period is often the first sign. Feeling tired or a little bloated is common.  
☐ Book your first visit with a gynaecologist  
☐ Ask your doctor about folic acid if you are not already taking it  

**Week 5** · a sesame seed (til)  
Baby: The neural tube, which becomes the brain and spine, is forming, and a simple heart has begun to beat.  
You: Nausea, tender breasts and needing to pass urine more often may begin now.  
☐ Write down every medicine and supplement you take, to show your doctor  

**Week 6** · a masoor dal grain  
Baby: Small buds that will become arms and legs appear. An early scan may show the heartbeat flickering.  
You: Morning sickness can happen at any time of day. Small, frequent meals often sit better.  
☐ Keep a light snack, like a few crackers, by your bed  

**Week 7** · a green pea (matar)  
Baby: The brain is growing quickly and the face is beginning to take shape.  
You: Strong smells may bother you, and some favourite foods may suddenly feel wrong.  
☐ Your doctor may suggest first blood tests, such as blood group, haemoglobin, sugar and thyroid  

**Week 8** · a rajma bean  
Baby: Fingers and toes are forming, and baby is already making small movements you cannot feel yet.  
You: Nausea and tiredness are often strongest around now. Rest when you can.  
☐ Ask whether you need a dating scan to confirm your due date  

**Week 9** · a grape (angoor)  
Baby: All the main organs have started to form. From now on, baby is mostly growing and maturing.  
You: Mood swings are common as hormones rise. Be gentle with yourself.  
☐ Keep a water bottle within reach through the day  

**Week 10** · a jamun  
Baby: Tiny nails are forming and the organs have begun to work.  
You: Your waistline may start to thicken, even before a bump shows.  
☐ Ask your doctor about the NT scan, usually done between 11 and 14 weeks  

**Week 11** · an amla (Indian gooseberry)  
Baby: Baby can open and close their hands, and the bones are starting to harden.  
You: For many women, nausea begins to ease over the next few weeks.  
☐ Have the NT scan and blood test if your doctor has advised them  

**Week 12** · a lime (nimbu)  
Baby: Reflexes are developing. Baby may curl their toes or startle at a touch on your tummy.  
You: The womb is rising out of the pelvis, so you may need the toilet a little less often.  
☐ Decide when you would like to share your news, in your own time  

**Week 13** · a chikoo (sapota)  
Baby: Fingerprints are forming. This is the last week of the first trimester.  
You: Energy often starts to return as you move into the second trimester.  
☐ Ask your doctor when to take the Td (tetanus-diphtheria) vaccine  

## Second trimester

**Week 14** · a mosambi (sweet lime)  
Baby: Baby can make small facial expressions, like squinting and frowning.  
You: Your appetite may come back. Simple, home-cooked meals are a good choice.  
☐ If your doctor agrees, start a gentle daily walk  

**Week 15** · an apple (seb)  
Baby: Baby can sense light through closed eyelids, and the skeleton keeps getting stronger.  
You: Gums may bleed a little when you brush. A soft toothbrush helps.  
☐ Book a dental check-up and tell the dentist you are pregnant  

**Week 16** · a guava (amrood)  
Baby: Neck and back muscles are stronger, so baby can hold their head more upright.  
You: Some women feel the first flutters now, often earlier in a second pregnancy.  
☐ Ask whether a quadruple marker blood test is advised for you  

**Week 17** · a pomegranate (anaar)  
Baby: A layer of fat is beginning to form under the skin, which will keep baby warm after birth.  
You: Brief twinges low in the belly are common as the ligaments stretch.  
☐ Try sleeping on your side with a pillow between your knees  

**Week 18** · a shakarkandi (sweet potato)  
Baby: The ears are in their final position and baby may hear muffled sounds.  
You: Feeling a little dizzy when you stand up quickly is common. Rise slowly.  
☐ Book the anomaly scan, usually done between 18 and 22 weeks  

**Week 19** · a shimla mirch (capsicum)  
Baby: A creamy coating called vernix is forming to protect baby's skin.  
You: Leg cramps, especially at night, may start. Gentle calf stretches can help.  
☐ Do a few gentle calf stretches before bed  

**Week 20** · a banana (kela)  
Baby: Halfway there. Baby swallows fluid and is practising for life outside.  
You: Your bump is probably showing now, and your navel may start to flatten.  
☐ Notice when you feel baby move, and when they seem most active  

**Week 21** · a carrot (gajar)  
Baby: Baby is swallowing and practising sucking.  
You: Movements may start to feel stronger and more regular.  
☐ Spend a few quiet minutes talking or reading to baby  

**Week 22** · a small papaya  
Baby: Eyebrows and eyelids have formed.  
You: Backache is common now. Gentle stretches and good posture can help.  
☐ Sit with your back supported and feet flat on the floor  

**Week 23** · a mango (aam)  
Baby: Baby can hear sounds from outside, a lovely time to read aloud.  
You: Swollen feet are common. Rest with your feet raised when you can.  
☐ Rest with your feet up for a few minutes each day  

**Week 24** · a bhutta (corn cob)  
Baby: The lungs are growing branches and tiny air sacs.  
You: The glucose test (GTT) is often done between weeks 24 and 28.  
☐ Book the glucose test (GTT) if your doctor has advised it  

**Week 25** · a cauliflower (phool gobhi)  
Baby: Baby responds to your voice and touch.  
You: Heartburn may increase. Smaller meals and not lying down right after eating can help.  
☐ Try smaller meals and wait a while before lying down  

**Week 26** · a baingan (brinjal)  
Baby: The eyes are beginning to open.  
You: As your bump grows, sleeping on your side may feel better.  
☐ Set up pillows to support your bump and back at night  

**Week 27** · a cabbage (patta gobhi)  
Baby: This is the last week of the second trimester.  
You: A good week to plan your third-trimester visits and tests with your doctor.  
☐ Ask your doctor how often you should visit in the third trimester  

## Third trimester

**Week 28** · a kheera (large cucumber)  
Baby: Baby can blink, and the brain is growing fast.  
You: Visits usually become more frequent from now on.  
☐ If your blood group is Rh negative, ask your doctor about the anti-D injection  
☐ Get to know baby's usual pattern of movements  

**Week 29** · a lauki (bottle gourd)  
Baby: Muscles and lungs keep maturing, and kicks may feel strong.  
You: You may feel short of breath as baby takes up more room.  
☐ Slow your pace on stairs and take breaks when you need them  

**Week 30** · a nariyal (coconut)  
Baby: The fine body hair (lanugo) is starting to disappear.  
You: Tiredness may return. Extra pillows can make sleep more comfortable.  
☐ Ask whether a growth scan is planned for you  

**Week 31** · an ananas (pineapple)  
Baby: All five senses are working, and baby turns their head from side to side.  
You: Practice tightenings (Braxton Hicks) may come and go. Mention them at your next visit.  
☐ Note down any questions to ask at your next visit  

**Week 32** · a kharbooja (muskmelon)  
Baby: Baby is practising breathing movements, and toenails have grown.  
You: Backache and disturbed sleep are common. Rest whenever you can.  
☐ Decide on your hospital and ask about visiting the labour ward  

**Week 33** · a small kathal (jackfruit)  
Baby: Bones are hardening, though the skull stays soft for birth.  
You: Hands and feet may swell a little. Remove tight rings if they feel snug.  
☐ Talk with your doctor about your wishes for the birth  

**Week 34** · a large papaya  
Baby: The lungs and nervous system are maturing well.  
You: You may feel more pressure low in your pelvis.  
☐ Plan who will be with you and help at home after the birth  

**Week 35** · a bunch of bananas  
Baby: The kidneys are fully developed. Most growth now is gaining weight.  
You: You may need the toilet more often again as baby moves lower.  
☐ Start packing your hospital bag  

**Week 36** · a small tarbooz (watermelon)  
Baby: Many babies settle head-down around now.  
You: Visits are often weekly from here. Breathing may ease as baby drops.  
☐ Keep your pregnancy file and ID together with the hospital bag  

**Week 37** · a petha (ash gourd)  
Baby: Baby is practising grasping and is getting ready for birth.  
You: Many women feel an urge to tidy and prepare the home.  
☐ Get a few baby clothes washed and ready  

**Week 38** · a kaddu (pumpkin)  
Baby: The fine body hair has mostly gone, and baby keeps gaining weight.  
You: Rest as much as you can. Short walks may still feel good if your doctor agrees.  
☐ Plan a little rest time every day  

**Week 39** · a large tarbooz (watermelon)  
Baby: Baby is fully ready. The brain keeps growing, even after birth.  
You: Waiting can feel long. Gentle routines help the days pass.  
☐ Do something calming you enjoy, like music or a short walk  

**Week 40** · a full-grown kathal (jackfruit)  
Baby: Your due date. Only a few babies arrive exactly on it.  
You: Follow your doctor's plan for the coming days.  
☐ Confirm the plan with your doctor if baby has not arrived yet  

**Week 41** · a full-grown kathal (jackfruit)  
Baby: Baby is still growing and keeping well inside.  
You: Your doctor may check on you and baby more often now.  
☐ Go for the extra check-ups your doctor suggests  

**Week 42** · a full-grown kathal (jackfruit)  
Baby: Baby is ready to meet you.  
You: Your doctor will guide the next steps for the birth.  
☐ Stay in close touch with your doctor
